# combat-choreography — audit card

Opened by `revision-pass` **Pass 4** when the module is on and the chapter contains physical
conflict. Skip in one line otherwise.

## The gate

> **What is different because this fight happened?**

A fight that changes nothing should not have been written. Cut it, or attach a consequence — an
injury, a witness, a position lost, a thing learned. This is the same question Pass 9 asks of the
chapter, asked of the scene that consumed the most words.

## Checks

- [ ] Could a reader draw the space at every moment? Positions, exits, what is between them
- [ ] There is a turn, and the fight after it is a different fight
- [ ] The terrain was used, not merely described
- [ ] Damage taken early changed what was possible later **in the same scene**
- [ ] The injuries that survive the scene are in the chapter's continuity facts, and will appear
      next chapter rather than healing silently
- [ ] The winner paid something
- [ ] No technique name is doing work that intention should do
- [ ] Nobody made a speech while fighting — people under load do not have the breath
- [ ] Fatigue is real: untrained bodies are finished in under a minute
- [ ] Sentences and paragraphs got shorter, and stayed short through the exchange

## Where it fails most often

| symptom | what it actually is | go to |
|---|---|---|
| Blow-by-blow with no turn | the scene has no structure, only duration | compress the routine; find where it changes |
| The content of the fight is who is stronger | tier is answering a question drama should | `power-scaling` |
| The MC holds back to teach a lesson | condescension the reader has to sit through | `mc-intel-meter` — nobody fights worse on purpose without a stated reason |
| Onlookers narrate how impressive it was | the crowd-reaction tic | `mtl-detox` |
| A new ability won it | it needed to be seen used and seen fail first | `power-system` |
| Killing cost nothing, or the same as last time | the first time is not like the others | `conflict-engine` |

## What it looks like when it lands

Two exchanges in a clause, then the moment he goes for the elbow — because the elbow ends it and
he wants it ended — gets the paragraph. She is on the wet ramp the whole time. Afterwards her
hand does not close properly, and it still does not, next chapter.
