# mtl-detox — audit card

Opened by `revision-pass` **Pass 7**, every chapter. `sw lint` searches the whole banned-phrase
list, counts exclamation marks outside dialogue and flags rhetorical questions in narration —
steps 1–3 of the detox pass. Those are mechanical and already done by the time you open this.

**Steps 4–8 are why this card exists.** No script can see a crowd-reaction block, price a
confrontation, or hear translationese in a rhythm. A clean `lint` run says nothing about any box
below.

- [ ] **No crowd-reaction block.** No paragraph in which a group reacts to the POV character —
      gasping, murmuring, reassessing, recognising. Delete it, or give the reaction to one named
      person who wanted something before the scene started
- [ ] **No rank recital or system lecture.** Every passage explaining the power system is in a
      character's mouth, for a reason, under 60 words. Power reaches the reader as cost and
      consequence (`power-system`, `power-scaling`)
- [ ] **No face-slap loop.** For every confrontation, name what it cost the winner. If the answer
      is nothing, the beat is the treadmill: arrogant nobody insults MC, MC reveals power, nobody
      grovels. Rewrite it so the win is paid for (`conflict-engine`)
- [ ] **Every antagonist present wants something legible and is competent at it.** If you cannot
      name both in one clause, fix the character before the sentence
- [ ] **No paragraph saying the same thing three ways.** Bulk translation pads by restatement;
      cut to the strongest version
- [ ] **Read the last 200 words aloud.** Translationese hides in rhythm — flat subject-verb-object
      chains of uniform length. Vary or rewrite

**What a hit is.** `lint` finds the string; you decide whether it is a defect and you rewrite the
sentence. Swapping in a synonym is the exact failure the banned list exists to prevent.

**The framing matters.** These are artifacts of bulk machine translation and web-serial incentive
structures — not of Chinese fiction, which at its best has none of them. Never let this pass turn
into a judgement about a culture or its literature; that is `bias-guard`'s territory and it
overrides genre convention.

If a box fails, open `SKILL.md` §Part 3, or `references/catalogues.md` for the full lists.

## What it looks like when it lands

The magistrate does say *"You dare?"* — and Bel, who is in the room, thinks it sounds like
something off a stage. One character's stock phrase, placed by somebody who can hear it as one.
The narration around it says nothing about how impressive anybody was.
