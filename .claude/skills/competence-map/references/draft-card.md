# competence-map — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 1**, every chapter. Written here rather than summarised there,
because who has to ask is a *plot* decision made before drafting — and once a scene is written with
a convenient mouth answering, the fix is a rewrite, not an edit.

**Produce:** the thing this chapter needs known, whose map actually covers it, and who must go to
someone else for it.

## The rule the skill rests on

**An unlisted domain is `none`, not "probably fine."** The instinct is to treat an unlisted domain
as adequate because adequacy keeps the scene moving. If this chapter needs a character to know
something not on their map, either they do not know it — *which is the scene* — or they learn it on
the page and it goes on the map.

Deep expertise budget: MC at most 3 domains above `professional`, other tier A at most 2, tier B at
most 1, tier C exactly 1 and it is their job.

## Pick the kind of not-knowing

"They don't know" is a wall. These are doors:

| kind | produces |
|---|---|
| **ignorant** | does not know the word; someone must explain, or they proceed blind |
| **wrong** | **the most useful.** Confident, outdated or folk model — they act competently on a false premise |
| **shallow** | knows the jargon, fails the second question |
| **rusty** | knew it once, in another era; the half that is wrong is dangerous |

**Prefer wrong to ignorant.** A character who does not know does nothing; a character who is wrong
does something, and the story is made of what people do.

## The referral

A specialist's most useful knowledge is often **who to ask**, and the asking costs — a favour owed,
a fee, an admission of ignorance to someone who will remember it. Delegation reads as *more*
capable, not less. Write the referral instead of an improvised second specialty.

Somebody in this chapter says they do not know, asks the person who does, or is visibly wrong.

## While drafting

Every stated fact carries a provenance: **taught · did · told · read · openly guessing.** If you
cannot name one, they cannot say the line. Foreknowledge is a sixth, bounded to the declared grain
and inventory — and it is never provenance for a *skill*.

Somebody learning something across chapters: `references/acquisition-ladder.md`. A god, immortal or
artificial mind with `knowledge_scope: broad`: `references/broad-knowledge.md`.
