# romance-arc

**Gate.** Applies only if `novel.md` → `optional.romance-arc` is `on`. Respect
`content.romance` (`none` / `subplot` / `central`) and `content.rating`. `no-harem` and
`bias-guard` apply regardless.

A romance is a plot: two people want something, are obstructed, and change. It is not a reward
schedule attached to the main plot.

**Who the lead is** is decided by `lead-interest` — the character-design half of this skill. Run
that first; this skill assumes a lead exists with their own goal, their own ladder, and a real
reason to refuse.

---

## Structure

Track the romantic line as its own thread in `state/threads.md` (`type: relationship`), with its
own beats and its own `due` chapters.

| beat | what happens | serial timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Meet** | a first impression that is *wrong* in an interesting way | early |
| **Friction** | they want different things and it matters | continuous |
| **Recognition** | one sees something in the other nobody else does | ~15% in |
| **Alliance** | they need each other for something not romantic | ~25% |
| **Vulnerability** | one shows the thing they hide; the other does not use it | ~40% |
| **Admission** | to themselves, not necessarily to the other | ~50% |
| **Obstacle** | the real one — values, loyalty, circumstance, not a misunderstanding | ~60% |
| **Cost** | one gives up something real for the other, off the balance sheet | ~75% |
| **Reversal** | it fails, or nearly does, for a reason that was always there | ~85% |
| **Resolution** | together or apart, changed either way | arc/novel end |

Scale to the novel: `subplot` spreads this across 100+ chapters and skips beats; `central`
gives each beat its own chapter.

## Rules

**The obstacle is never a misunderstanding that a single conversation would fix.** That is the
weakest device in the form and readers name it immediately. Real obstacles: incompatible loyalties,
a genuine values conflict, a cost one is unwilling to pay, someone else who would be hurt,
different timelines for their lives.

**Both people want something else too.** A character whose entire arc is the romance has no arc.
The romance should *cost* them progress on their other goal — that is what makes it matter.

**Intimacy escalates in trust, not in contact.** The strongest beats are: telling the truth about
something small · being seen doing something they are bad at · being trusted with a task ·
noticing an absence · not explaining and being understood.

**Attraction is specific.** Name what each notices. Physical description alone is
interchangeable; a habit, a competence, a moment of unexpected decency is not.

**Let them be wrong about each other.** First impressions that are half right produce fifty
chapters of material.

**Do not resolve early.** In a serial, the answered question loses tension. Resolve the *stage*,
not the relationship: an admission opens the question of what to do about it.

**After resolution, the relationship is still a plot.** Established couples argue, misalign, and
have to choose each other again under new pressure. A romance that goes quiet after the kiss was
a reward, not a plot.

## Content

Follow `content.rating`. At `teen`, intimacy ends at the door; at `mature`, it may be on the page
but the requirement is unchanged: **the scene must do character work.** What changes between them
is the content of the scene. Never write intimacy involving minors, in any framing, at any rating.

Consent is legible and mutual on the page — not as a disclaimer, but because coercion written as
romance is one of the corpus defects this toolkit exists to remove.

## Self-check

- [ ] The romantic line is a thread with beats and `due` chapters
- [ ] The current obstacle is not a fixable misunderstanding
- [ ] Both parties have goals outside the relationship, active this arc
- [ ] The last three romantic beats escalated trust, not just proximity
- [ ] What each finds specifically compelling in the other is on the page
- [ ] `no-harem` and `bias-guard` checks pass
