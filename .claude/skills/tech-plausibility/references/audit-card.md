# tech-plausibility — audit card

Opened by `revision-pass` **Pass 5** in science fiction. Three failures account for almost all of
them, and only the first is obvious.

## The gate

> **Why is the technology not solving this scene?**

Ask it of the chapter's central obstacle. If the honest answer is "because then there would be no
chapter", the obstacle is wrong, not the technology. A capability that appears only when the plot
wants it is the defect readers name as "convenient", and it is unrecoverable once noticed: the
reader begins auditing every scene.

## Checks

- [ ] Nothing was resolved by a capability the setting had not already established
- [ ] The opposition has comparable access to the same tech tree, and used it
- [ ] Capabilities and costs are what they were in earlier chapters; any change is a plot event
      somebody caused, not a drift
- [ ] Nobody explained their own tools to someone who lives with them
- [ ] Characters are **bored** by their own world — the wonder is the reader's, and it arrives
      through precision rather than awe
- [ ] The chapter showed the **interface**, not the mechanism: the gesture, the wait, the
      annoyance
- [ ] At most one new term, and it is in `bible/lexicon.md` with a plain-English meaning
- [ ] Hardness level held — a soft setting is not an inconsistent one
- [ ] Any artificial mind stayed inside its declared boundary and its bounded access channel

## The one to look for when everything above passes

**The poor version is missing.** A chapter set among people who can afford the good technology,
with no sign of what the cheap one does to somebody, is the genre's commonest thinness. It is
usually one clause: what broke, who is still using the old thing, what it costs to go without.

Second-order effects belong in `bible/society.md` — `social-fabric` owns the propagation, and a
chapter that treats the speculation as scenery rather than as something people live inside is a
`world-texture` failure before it is one of these.
