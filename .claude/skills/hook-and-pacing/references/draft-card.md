# hook-and-pacing — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 1**, every chapter. Written here rather than summarised there,
because the opening and the hook are the two positions that re-earn a reader who is following six
other stories, and both are chosen before drafting, not discovered afterwards.

**Produce:** the opening-line strategy and the closing hook type.

## Opening — you have two sentences

**Never:** weather · waking up · a recap paragraph · an encyclopaedia entry · a rank recital · a
timestamp alone · a character reflecting on the last chapter.

| type | shape |
|---|---|
| Mid-argument | a line of dialogue already in conflict |
| Action in progress | the verb is already happening |
| Wrong detail | something present that should not be, stated flatly |
| Declarative with a story inside it | *The coat did not fit and she wore it anyway.* |
| Consequence first | open on the aftermath, fill in backwards inside the scene |
| Contradiction of the last hook | the hook promised a fight; open on the two of them eating |

**Re-anchoring is one clause inside a sentence doing other work** — never a paragraph, never "as
you'll recall". If more than a clause is needed, the previous chapter's hook was unclear.

## Hook — an unresolved pressure, not a cliffhanger

**Revelation · Arrival · Decision · Question** are the workhorses. **Threat** moderate,
**Reversal** occasional, **Quiet** once per arc after a heavy chapter, **Cliffhanger max once per
8–10 chapters**.

- Rotate the type: it differs from the previous two chapters.
- End on the **last strong beat**, not the wind-down after it — three paragraphs past the right
  stopping point is the usual failure.
- Concrete. *"Something was wrong"* is not a hook; *"Her father's name was the first line in the
  ledger"* is.
- **Hook honesty**: deliver what you implied, or something better — never something smaller.

## Length is not a quality metric

Nothing here gates on `chapters.length_band`. **Never pad, and never trim to a number.** A chapter
that comes out short is diagnosed, not padded: the turn happened offstage, the cost was skipped, or
a scene ended at its first obstacle instead of its consequence.

Pace varies *within* the chapter: medium open → fast through the turn → slow for the
follow-through → fast for the hook.
