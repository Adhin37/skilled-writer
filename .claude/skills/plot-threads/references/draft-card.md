# plot-threads — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 1**, every chapter. Written here rather than summarised there,
because thread ops are decided against the whole open ledger, and a chapter drafted first and
reconciled afterwards is how promises get quietly abandoned.

**Produce:** the thread ops firing this chapter, in CCS notation, for the `thr>` line.

| op | notation | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| open | `~T14` | the promise is made — noticeable without being announced |
| advance | `^T03` | new information, higher stakes, or a step closer. Does not resolve |
| pay | `vT09` | the promise is kept |
| abandon | `xT21` | deliberate retirement, with a written reason and usually an on-page acknowledgement |

The read-set carries the open threads with their ages. Decide against it, not from memory.

## What the ledger wants from this chapter

- **5–9 open.** Below 4 the story reads as episodic; above 12 nothing feels urgent.
- **All three tiers alive** — 1–2 novel-level, 2–3 arc-level, 3–4 chapter-level. The chapter-level
  tier is what makes a serial feel like it is moving; if readers say it drags, that tier is empty.
- **Anything past its `due`** gets a reason in `carried` and a new `due`, in this pass. A thread
  carried twice is escalated on the page or abandoned on the page.
- **A payoff here** was planted at least twice, earlier, and does not explain its own foreshadowing.

**What counts as payment:** the promise the reader was made, answered. A mystery paid by revealing
another mystery has been renamed, not paid.

## Opening arc only

Four promise-ledger threads are due earlier than anything else in the book — the world anchor, the
genre contract, `opening.promise` touched on the page, and the advantage's first legible win. They
are promises to a reader who has not yet decided to keep reading, so they cannot be paid late:
`plot-threads` §The promise ledger — arc 1 only.

Planting a setup, or auditing for forgotten promises: `references/foreshadowing.md`.
