# grimdark-consequences — audit card

Opened by `revision-pass` **Pass 9**, with delivery, when the module is on. Two gates that fail in
opposite directions, which is why both are here.

## Gate 1 — has anything been undone?

> **Read the chapter against the last five CCS blocks. Is anything that was paid now unpaid?**

A wound that stopped mattering, a death somebody has recovered from too easily, a moral cost the
narrative has quietly forgiven, a loss that turns out to have been optimal. Each is a small
retroactive rescue, and they accumulate into a story where consequence is decorative.

## Gate 2 — is anything left to lose?

> **Name the thing the reader is afraid of losing.**

If you cannot, this chapter has nothing at stake, however much it took. Tension here comes from
the reader's belief that the story will not protect anyone — which requires somebody they still
hope for.

## Checks

- [ ] Every persistent injury from earlier chapters appeared, or was plausibly absent
- [ ] This arc's victory is partial and the shortfall has a name
- [ ] Nobody who died was disposable — they wanted something, and it is on the page
- [ ] The chapter had a goal and a turn, not only a loss
- [ ] Competence improved the outcome somewhere
- [ ] Grief is specific and short rather than extended
- [ ] Nothing depicted suffering as spectacle (`bias-guard` governs this absolutely)
- [ ] No shock reversal broke the story's own logic (`plot-threads` still binds)
- [ ] The MC's intelligence is unchanged — a grim world does not license a stupid protagonist

## What it looks like when it lands

Ivar's hand does not come back, and he was the one who could write. The victory holds — the wagons
are through — and the shortfall has a name, which is the eleven who are not. The boy is still
alive, and the reader is now afraid for him.
