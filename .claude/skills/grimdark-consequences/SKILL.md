# grimdark-consequences

**Gate.** Applies only if `novel.md` → `optional.grimdark-consequences` is `on`.

This is not a licence for cruelty. It is a promise to the reader: **what happens, stays
happened.** Tension in this mode comes from the reader's justified belief that the story will not
protect anyone.

---

## The rules

1. **Named characters can die**, including ones the reader is attached to, including at moments
   that are not narratively convenient. At least one death per arc that the reader did not see
   coming and that solves nothing.

2. **Injuries persist.** Recovery takes chapters, not paragraphs, and some things do not come
   back. Log every lasting injury in the character's continuity facts; a limp mentioned once and
   forgotten is worse than no limp.

3. **Victories are partial.** The MC saves most of them, or the right ones, or none. A clean win
   belongs at most once per novel and should be immediately expensive.

4. **Moral costs stay paid.** A character who does something unforgivable is not forgiven by the
   narrative. Others treat them differently. They treat themselves differently. It appears in
   their voice deltas (`character-development`).

5. **No retroactive rescue.** Nobody was secretly alive. The sacrifice was not unnecessary. This
   is the single fastest way to void the reader's trust, and it cannot be re-earned.

6. **Institutions do not reward virtue.** The right thing is done at a cost, sometimes to no
   effect, and the story does not arrange for it to have been secretly optimal.

7. **Consequences reach the MC's choices**, not just their circumstances. The bad outcome they
   caused is the one the reader will remember.

## What keeps it readable

Unrelieved bleakness is as monotonous as unrelieved triumph, and readers leave for the same
reason: nothing is at risk when everything is lost.

- **Preserve something the reader wants protected.** Grimdark works because there is something to
  lose. If everything is already ash, no stake registers.
- **Small mercies are load-bearing.** A kindness that costs the giver something, and holds, is
  what makes the losses land.
- **Forward motion.** Every chapter still has a goal and a turn. Suffering is not a plot; a
  character *choosing* under pressure is.
- **Competence is still rewarded.** Skill and preparation should improve outcomes, or the reader
  disengages from the MC's decisions entirely.
- **Grief is specific and brief on the page.** A paragraph of the wrong practical detail beats
  three pages of mourning.

## Not this

- Suffering as spectacle, particularly sexual violence — see `bias-guard`; it stays off the page
  and its consequences belong to the person it happened to
- Cynicism as a substitute for theme
- Characters who exist to be killed for effect — everyone who dies wanted something
- Nihilism that makes choices meaningless: if nothing matters, there is no story
- Shock reversals that break the internal logic; grimdark still obeys `plot-threads`

## Interaction

Raises the severity band in `conflict-engine`: the "large" cost tier fires every arc without
exception, and the "defining" tier fires roughly twice as often. `character-development`
regression conditions trigger more readily. `mc-intel-meter` is unchanged — a grim world does not
license a stupid protagonist.

## Self-check

- [ ] Nothing that happened has been undone
- [ ] Every persistent injury is in continuity facts and appears when relevant
- [ ] This arc's victory was partial, and the shortfall has a name
- [ ] Nobody who died was disposable
- [ ] There is still something the reader is afraid of losing
- [ ] The chapter had a goal and a turn, not just a loss
