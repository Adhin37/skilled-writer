# character-profile — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 1**, whenever anyone new is on the page. Written here rather than
summarised there, because the tier is decided **before they speak** — a walk-on who gets a
psychology mid-draft is how a 400-chapter serial runs out of context.

**Produce:** the cast tier of anyone new, and for a tier C, their three strokes.

## The default is C. Promote on evidence, never in anticipation.

| tier | who | record |
|---|---|---|
| **A** principal | anyone whose beliefs the story is *about* | full file, ~600 w |
| **B** supporting | recurs across an arc and does a job, but has no arc of their own | short file, ~150 w |
| **C** walk-on | one to three scenes and gone | one line in `_extras.md`, ~25 w |
| **D** furniture | the guard who says *halt* | nothing |

A tier-C character does not need a Lie, a ladder, a fear or a childhood. **They need to not be
interchangeable**, which is a different problem with a much cheaper solution.

## Tier C — three strokes, invented on the spot

1. **A want inside the scene** — what they are trying to get through in the next five minutes.
   *Close early. Not be blamed. Get the cart moving.* A five-minute goal, never a life goal.
2. **One habit**, physical or verbal, small and repeatable. *Stamps twice and checks the second.*
3. **One piece of the world they carry** — a price, a grievance, a rule they enforce, something that
   changed at their work last month. **This is the stroke that matters**: it turns a function into a
   window onto the world, and it is where thread seeds and world texture enter without an exposition
   scene. It is also their **entire competence map** — the toll clerk knows the levy and not why it
   was raised.

Plus **one axis off default**, taken off `voice-separation`'s matrix. Without it they speak in
the narrator's register, and the narrator's register is the MC's.

**The swap test:** could this scene be handed to any other extra with no edit? If yes, fix stroke 3.

**No interiority below tier B.** What a walk-on wants is visible in what they do with their hands.

**The doomed-extra rule.** Someone written to die next chapter gets the *same* three strokes as any
other walk-on, delivered in the chapter **before** the one where they matter. Sudden
characterization is a death flag and readers read it fluently.

Do not stop to build a profile mid-draft — sketch them, and log the roster line in Step 5. If they
hit a third appearance or changed the plot, they are promoted then, from what is already on the page.

`bias-guard` applies at every tier and is *most* often broken at tier C, where a person is one
detail wide and the default reaches for a stereotype to fill the space.
