# write-chapter — the shape of a chapter, and re-anchoring

Open this in Phase B when you are laying out the chapter: the proportions a chapter is built to,
what belongs in the cold open, and how much of a recap a serial reader is owed. The brief already
fixed the scene split; this is the shape that split sits in.

### Chapter anatomy

Proportions, not word counts:

| cold open ~7% | scene 1 ~40% | `* * *` | scene 2 ~40% | hook ~5% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| in motion — dialogue, action, or a wrong-feeling detail. Never weather, waking up or a recap | goal, obstacle, first turn | | consequence of the turn. **The delivery lands here** | the last beat (`hook-and-pacing`) |

**Chapter 1 has its own shape** — disruption, investment, world, cliffhanger — in
`story-opening/references/chapter-one.md`. Use that instead inside the opening arc.

Across those sections, **25–40% of the words are spoken aloud**. Deviate freely when the material
wants it — but never end without a hook if `chapters.hook_required` is true.

### Recap discipline

Readers arrive a day or a week later. Re-anchor in **one clause, inside a sentence doing other
work**: "The coat she'd taken off Dael's body still smelled of the undercroft." Never a paragraph,
never "As you'll remember".
