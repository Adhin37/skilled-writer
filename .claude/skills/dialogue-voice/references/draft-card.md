# dialogue-voice — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 2**, every chapter, while drafting. Written here rather than
summarised there, because the share of spoken words is the gate every other voice check depends on:
if the cast barely speaks, the tests in this toolkit silently no-op.

**Hold while writing:** the dialogue share, each speaker's fingerprint, and one subtext exchange.

## How much

**Target 25–40% of the chapter's words inside `"…"`** — spoken aloud, to another person.

| share | reads as | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| under 10% | interior monologue with quotes attached | **defect** — the cast is scenery |
| 10–20% | literary-introspective | occasional, deliberate chapters only |
| 25–40% | the serialized format | target |
| over 55% | radio play; setting and interiority starve | pull back |

**Speech only.** Thought `'…'` and meta `[…]` do not count — a chapter can be 40% marked text and
still be one person alone in their head, which is the exact defect this catches.

**The diagnostic:** an analytical POV voice is the usual cause, because it is pleasant to write and
it quietly eats the scene. The tell is a second person physically present who says nothing, or says
one line and is narrated *about* for three paragraphs. When two people are in a room, the beat
belongs to what they **say** to each other.

## Per speaker

Load the matrix row, the fingerprint, and the accumulated voice deltas from `state/growth.md`.
Tier A gets all eight fields; tier B gets contractions, vocabulary tell and syntax tic; a walk-on
gets their five-minute want, their tic, and one thing only someone in their job would say.

**Hold the turn lengths** — the crudest differentiator and the most reliable. **One voice per line**:
a line that could move to another character with no edit is generic.

Beats come from that character's **hands** and **pressure move**, never from the default set —
*nodded, shrugged, sighed, raised an eyebrow, crossed their arms, let out a breath*. A line with no
beat beats a line with a generic one. Tags are `said`/`asked`; an action beat beats an adverb.

**Nobody explains a subject their competence map does not cover**, and no two people who both know
a thing explain it to each other. *"I don't know"*, *"that's not my end"* and *"I'd guess, but it's
a guess"* read as competence, not weakness.

## One subtext exchange per scene

Before a loaded exchange, write two lines per speaker: **wants** (what they are asking this
conversation for) and **won't say** (the thing they will not put in words, and why). If `won't say`
is empty for both, the scene needs no subtext. If it is empty for every scene, the chapter is a
briefing.

Techniques: **contradicting action** (the body is believed over the words) · **evasion** (where they
dodge is the information) · **irony** · **the displaced object** (nominally about the roof, actually
about the marriage). **Never gloss it** — if the reader might miss it, add a beat, not a sentence of
interpretation.

Two characters will not separate however hard you push: `references/corpus-anti-patterns.md`.
