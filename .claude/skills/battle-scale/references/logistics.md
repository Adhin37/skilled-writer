# battle-scale — the campaign

Open this when the chapter covers the movement around a battle rather than the battle: a march, a
siege, a winter, a retreat. Or when an engagement needs a reason to have gone the way it did that
is not somebody being braver.

**The premise: battles are the visible part of a logistical argument that was mostly settled
beforehand.** A story that treats them as isolated set pieces has thrown away its best source of
pressure, because supply is a deadline that nobody can argue with.

---

## The four constraints

| constraint | the question | what it gives the story |
|---|---|---|
| **Food** | how many days' worth, and where does the next lot come from | the hardest deadline in fiction — it does not negotiate and it cannot be postponed |
| **Water** | how far from it can they get, and how long to refill | decides the route, which decides where the battle happens |
| **Movement** | how far a day, in this weather, on this road, with the wagons | makes distance load-bearing and stops plans that violate the map |
| **Money** | who is being paid, and when were they last paid | armies dissolve over arrears more reliably than over defeat |

Write the numbers down once, in `bible/world.md` beside the distance table, and then never let a
plan violate them. A force that arrives fed, dry, on time and unpaid-for has been moved by the
plot rather than by anybody's decisions.

## What actually destroys armies

In rough order, and the first two are almost never in fiction:

1. **Disease and exposure.** Any force sitting still is losing people steadily, and a besieging
   army is usually sicker than the besieged. This is where the attrition comes from in a campaign
   that has not fought.
2. **Desertion.** People go home for the harvest, because they are not being paid, or because
   somebody said the war was over. Desertion is a *decision*, which makes it characterisation.
3. **Straggling.** Anyone who cannot keep the pace is gone as surely as a casualty, and the
   veterans know exactly how far behind the stragglers are.
4. **Running out.** Then foraging, which means taking food from people who need it — the cheapest
   way to put the war's morality in front of a sympathetic POV character without an argument
   about it.
5. **Battle**, which is the fast and dramatic way, and the least common.

## Sieges

A siege is a **waiting scene with a clock**, and the clock is the point.

Both sides are starving on different schedules; whoever is better supplied is winning, however it
looks. The garrison knows the day the food runs out, and one of them has told somebody. The
besiegers know the relief force is coming and roughly when. Nothing needs to be assaulted for the
chapter to have a turn: a well going bad, a deserter's story, a change in the ration, a gate
somebody has decided to open.

The assault, if it comes, is the failure of the arithmetic — an admission by one side that they
have run out of time. Write it as that, and it lands far harder than a set piece.

## The occupied and the paid

The half of a campaign a serial usually skips, and the half a reader remembers:

- **Somebody's field is the camp.** They are not compensated. They are still there afterwards.
- **Prices move.** A war is a demand shock; grain, iron, horses and boots all cost differently
  within a week, and somebody is getting rich (`social-fabric/references/prices-and-stakes.md`).
- **News travels at its own speed** and arrives distorted. The version three towns away is a
  different event, and people are making decisions on it — `social-fabric` owns how fast news moves.
- **The wounded go somewhere.** Who feeds them. Who is expected to take them in. What happens to
  the ones who cannot work afterwards — that is the war the civilians actually experience.

## How much of this reaches the page

Very little of it, and as friction rather than as information. `world-texture`'s budget still
binds: the reader learns the army is short of food because a sergeant is lying about the ration,
not because a paragraph says supply was strained. One concrete detail — what they are eating, and
what they were eating a week ago — does more than a page of quartermastery.

The rest of it is for you, so that the plans in the chapter are constrained by something. That is
the whole return on this file: a deadline nobody in the story chose.
