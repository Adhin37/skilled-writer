# battle-scale — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` Phase A when the module is on **and the chapter contains an engagement
larger than a fight between people who can all see each other**. A skirmish is
`combat-choreography`; close this card in one line.

**Produce four lines for the brief:**

```
frame     the body - Renna's hundred metres of the ford, and nothing else
objective hold the crossing until the wagons are past. She does not know how many wagons
losing    the road open, the town by evening, her sister in it
channel   a horn she can hear, and a runner who will arrive late and out of breath
```

## The four

1. **The frame**, chosen once and held: the **body** (one person's hundred metres), the
   **decision** (a commander inside their bad information), or the **line** (one unit's job).
   Mixing them is what makes battle prose read as a summary of somebody else's game.
2. **The objective**, in plain words. One thing this person is trying to do.
3. **What losing looks like**, concretely, for them — not "defeat" but what defeat reaches.
4. **The channel.** How they would find out what is happening elsewhere. Battles run on
   information, and a character who simply knows the state of the field has been handed
   omniscience (`competence-map`).

## Then decide the attrition

Before drafting, not while. Roughly what fraction of this unit is gone by the end — and **which
one casualty has a name the reader already knows.** The fraction is why the engagement matters;
the name is why anyone feels it.

## While drafting

- **Scale arrives through sense, never through numbers.** Time for an order to travel. The noise.
  What people are eating. The wing you only hear about.
- **No establishing paragraph of dispositions.** It is the rank recital in uniform (`mtl-detox`),
  and it is where readers stop.
- **The outcome reaches the reader the way it reaches the character** — late, partial, and from
  somebody who may be wrong.
- **Most of it is waiting.** Put the character work there.
- Cohesion decides it, not casualties: the question is which side stops being an organisation
  first.
- An exceptional character changes the result **through other people** — the line holds because
  somebody was seen. `power-scaling` still binds.

If the frame will not settle, open `battle-scale/SKILL.md` §1. For the campaign around the
engagement — supply, movement, why armies actually lose — `references/logistics.md`.
