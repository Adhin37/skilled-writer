# social-perception — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` Phase A. One decision, and in a chapter with no meaningful contact
between people the answer is `reads: nothing` and the card closes.

**Produce two lines for the brief:**

```
reads    Halden is calm, so the deal is already done - she is tier 3 and does not revisit a read
wrong    he is calm because he has decided to refuse. She finds out at the door
```

## The decision

**What does the POV character conclude about somebody, and is it right?**

Take their `eq` from `bible/cast/_voices.md` and hold it. A tier-2 observer notices that something
is off and guesses self-referentially. A tier-4 observer reads intent and is still guessing at
why. Nobody at any tier reads a face perfectly — what rises with tier is the quality of the
question, not the accuracy of the answer.

## If the answer is "wrong", all four must be true

1. The **signal was on the page**, in this chapter, where the reader could see it.
2. The reading is the one **their tier and their history would produce** — reasonable, not a
   lapse.
3. The **true cause is also on the page**, in something the reader saw and this character did
   not weight.
4. It **costs** something. Otherwise it is a texture beat, not a failure.

That is the fourth legal way to make an intelligent character fail, beside missing information,
opposed will and cost (`mc-intel-meter` owns those three).

## While drafting

- **State a reading, never a mind.** *She had decided something before she came in* is what this
  character can see. *She had decided to refuse him* is somebody else's head.
- **Use the signature, not the gesture set.** Each character's fear, anger, grief and relief look
  like a specific thing in their profile. If the beat you reach for is a nod, a sigh or a
  released breath, it belongs to nobody (`voice-separation`).
- **The POV's own feelings ride in free indirect discourse**, unmarked, as always
  (`narrator-voice`). This card is about the other people in the room.
- Somebody with a **higher** `eq` than the POV character is reading *them*, and can act on it
  before the POV knows they were read.

If the decision will not settle, open `social-perception/references/reading-people.md`.
