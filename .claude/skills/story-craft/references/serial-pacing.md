# Serial pacing — the webnovel layer

Open this when setting release cadence or arc temperature, when planning the opening chapters of a
serial, or when the question is retention rather than craft.

A serialized webnovel is not a novel published in pieces. The reader decides whether to continue
after every chapter, and the structure has to survive that.

## The numbers that shape everything

- **Roughly half of readers drop after chapter 1.** That is the single hardest constraint in the
  format, and it is why `story-opening` exists as its own skill.
- Serial fiction "has to work twice as hard just to keep the reader hooked" than a bookstore novel,
  because there is no sunk cost — nobody paid, and the next story is one click away.
- Arc breaks are the second-largest drop-off point. Open the next arc's question before the current
  one closes.

## Three pacing layers, not one

Balance them the way a television series balances episode, season and show:

| layer | unit | question |
|---|---|---|
| chapter | one release | did something happen that a reader would miss if they skipped it? |
| arc | ~25 chapters | does the tension have a shape, or is it flat until the last two chapters? |
| novel | the whole | is the pressure still rising, and has the promise been kept? |

A story can be well paced at one layer and badly paced at another. The common serial failure is a
chapter-level pace that is fine and an arc-level pace that is flat.

## Slow burn without losing the reader

Slow burn is viable — it is not the same as slow. What makes it work:

- **Something interesting happens in every chapter**, even a quiet one. Quality and plotting are how
  a slow-paced serial keeps retention; there is no other mechanism.
- **Low-key chapters build elements that later combine.** The build-up is *deposits*, and the payoff
  is the withdrawal. A quiet chapter that deposits nothing is filler.
- **Set expectations early.** If the opening promises a scale the next twenty chapters will not
  deliver, the reader who liked the promise leaves. A reader who wants explosions should be able to
  self-select out by chapter 3 — that is the genre contract, and it protects the slow burn as much
  as it protects the reader.
- **Alternate.** Balance exciting beats with calmer sections that introduce characters, answer a
  mystery, or move a relationship. The calm section is where build-up happens.

## Cadence

Daily is the gold standard on the major platforms; three times a week is a common sustainable rate.
The choice that matters is one you can hold — an audience grows around a predictable schedule and
punishes gaps harder than it punishes a slower rate.

## What this means for build-up

Build-up and retention are usually posed as opposites. They are not, and the resolution is the same
one in `SKILL.md`: build-up is the same beats delivered as scene rather than summary. A quiet
chapter that plays a beat has a want, a turn and a cost, and passes the skim test. A quiet chapter
that reports beats is filler and the skim test correctly kills it.
