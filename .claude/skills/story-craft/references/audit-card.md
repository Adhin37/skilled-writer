# story-craft — audit card

Opened by `revision-pass` **Pass 9f**. Pass 9 asks whether the chapter *delivers*; this asks whether
it delivered at the **right size** — the failure a delivery test cannot see, because a chapter that
summarises its own turning point still has a want, a friction, a change, a cost and a hook.

`sw lint` prints a **pacing** line — summary markers per 1,000 words, words before the first scene,
and the summary-shaped paragraph share. It says where to look. It is a diagnostic and not a target.

- [ ] **The chapter's most important beat is in scene.** Name it, then find it on the page. If it is
      reported rather than played, the chapter is rushing regardless of its length
- [ ] **No past-perfect clause carries a turn.** Ordinary past-perfect is how English orders two past
      events and is fine. The defect is a plot event inside one — *"she had spent three weeks making
      it true"* is a campaign that happened offstage
- [ ] **Everything in summary is a bridge.** Travel, established repetition, time in which nothing
      turned. If a summarised stretch contains a cost or a change, it wanted a scene
- [ ] **Nothing the reader is later asked to feel happened offstage**
- [ ] **A win was bought.** The failure that earned it was on the page, not in a clause
- [ ] **Opening arc only:** the reader watched the ordinary world work before it broke; each person
      was met before they carried a scene; a rule bit somebody before it bit the protagonist

**Do not read a low summary-marker count as a pass.** A chapter can skip its most important beat
without using a single past-perfect clause — by starting after it. The first box is the pass; the
markers only tell you where to look first.

**Do not fix this by adding words.** Length is a measured fact here and is scored on nothing. The
repair is always reallocation: a beat that was reported becomes a beat that is played, and a bridge
that was played goes back to being a bridge.

If a box fails, open `story-craft/SKILL.md` §The one decision, or
`story-craft/references/scene-and-summary.md`.
