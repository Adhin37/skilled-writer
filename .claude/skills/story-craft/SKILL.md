# story-craft

This skill owns the layer the rest of the toolkit does not have: **the shape of a story over time**,
and the one decision that controls it — whether a beat is played as a scene or reported as summary.

`scene-craft` owns what happens *inside* a scene. `hook-and-pacing` owns, in its own words, "the
first 100 words and the last 100". Between them sat nothing, and that gap is where a story goes
fast to the finish line.

## The one decision: scene or summary

Every beat in a story is delivered one of two ways.

- A **scene** happens in front of the reader: a specific time and place, moment by moment, action
  and dialogue and a turn. The reader experiences it.
- A **summary** reports that it happened. The reader is told.

Both are necessary. Scenes create investment; summary creates momentum between them. The question
is never "more scene" — it is **which beats get which**.

**A beat earns a scene when any of these is true:**

- something changes that the reader will need to have watched
- somebody pays a cost
- the reader meets a person, a place or a rule for the first time
- it is the first time a thing works, or the first time it fails
- the story will later ask the reader to feel the weight of it

**A beat takes summary when it is a bridge**: travel, repetition the reader has already understood,
elapsed time in which nothing turns, the fourth instance of something established on the first.

### The failure this toolkit actually has

It is **one-directional**. Machine-written fiction does not usually over-elaborate trivia; it
**gives the important beat to summary** and moves on. The measured signature is a plot event
carried in a past-perfect subordinate clause:

> *"She had made this exact lie four times in six weeks."*
> *"She had spent three weeks making it true."*

Four attempts and three weeks of scheming — the build-up of the entire opening — reported in two
clauses. That is not concision. It is the story's own setup happening offstage, and the reader
arrives at the fifth attempt with no reason to care about it.

**The test.** Find every past-perfect clause in the chapter. For each, ask: *is a turn happening
inside this sentence?* If yes, a scene was skipped. Either play it, or cut the reference and let
the reader meet the thing fresh — but do not report a turn.

## What build-up is

**Build-up is not less happening. It is the same beats delivered as scene instead of summary.**

This matters because the rest of the toolkit is right and must not be relaxed. `conflict-engine`
says every chapter costs something and zero is not on the scale. `scene-craft` says the exit state
may not equal the entry state. The skim test says a chapter a reader could skip is filler. All true.
Those four earlier lies each had a want, a friction, a change, a cost and a hook — **they would have
passed every one of those gates as chapters.** They were simply never played.

So build-up never means a slower chapter. It means:

- the reader **watches the ordinary thing work** before it breaks
- the reader **meets a person** before that person is asked to carry a scene
- a cost is **paid small** before it is paid large
- a rule **bites somebody** before it bites the protagonist

All four are delivered in scene, with a want and a turn. None of them is an establishing paragraph,
a gazetteer sentence or a history lecture — which is why nothing here licenses those, and why the
ban on them does not license skipping this.

## The shape

- **A win must be bought.** A protagonist who tries and fails, under worse conditions each time,
  earns the success. The pattern is attempt → failure → escalation, and outcomes are "yes, but" or
  "no, and" far more often than plain success. Two failures before a significant win is a floor,
  not a stylistic choice.
- **Setup earns the conflict that follows.** Proportion matters less than function: a long opening
  works if it earns what comes after, and a short one fails if it does not.
- **Pressure rises from the midpoint.** The midpoint turns the story rather than resolving it. The
  common machine failure is to introduce the setback and the climax briefly and settle both quickly,
  which flattens the whole arc.
