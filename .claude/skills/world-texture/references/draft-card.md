# world-texture — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 1**, every chapter. Written here rather than summarised there,
because the world channel is a decision — *which one thing about the world does this chapter make
concrete, and what carries it* — and a chapter that skips the decision reaches for channel 4 by
reflex and becomes a travelogue.

**Produce:** the one thing about the world this chapter makes concrete, and which channel carries it.

## The four channels, cheapest first

| # | channel | cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Consequence** — a rule of the world bites someone in this scene | free; it *is* the plot |
| 2 | **Friction** — a character works around the world to get what they want | free; it *is* the obstacle |
| 3 | **Assumed reference** — a thing mentioned as obvious, unexplained | ~free; one clause |
| 4 | **Direct description** — narration states what a place is like | expensive |

```
flat        The market was crowded and dangerous, full of merchants from many lands.
channel 1   The toll-taker weighed her coat, decided it was worth a copper, and took it.
channel 2   She went the long way around the fountain. Fountains had ears.
channel 3   "Not on a levy day," he said, and that was the end of the plan.
```

**Two beats of channels 1–3 for every sentence of channel 4.** If you cannot name them, you are
decorating. **The fix for a flat world is never "add description"** — it is more consequence, more
friction, the same detail count doing work.

## The budget, per scene and per chapter

Direct description **≤8% of the chapter's words** · sensory details **2–4 per scene**, at least one
non-visual, placed in motion and never in a block · **3 consecutive descriptive sentences maximum**,
once per chapter · 2–3 new proper nouns · ≤1 new invented term, used twice or cut · **zero**
paragraphs of world history — history reaches the reader as a character's grudge.

The opening arc owes the reader an anchor, and that debt is paid through channels 1–3, **not** by
raising the channel-4 budget.

## Anchors

Each location carries a two-detail sensory signature in `bible/world.md`, at least one non-visual.
**Repeat it verbatim-ish at every visit, inside the first 100 words of arriving** — repetition is the
whole mechanism by which a name becomes a place. A location already visited gets *fewer* details,
not the same ones re-explained: its anchor, and only what has changed.

Every detail must characterise the noticer, set up a later beat, anchor a location, carry a social
fact, or create friction. The best do two.

The world reads flat, or bloated: `references/overbuilding.md`.
