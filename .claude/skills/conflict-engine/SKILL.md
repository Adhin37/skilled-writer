# conflict-engine

The rule the whole toolkit rests on: **nothing is free.** A serial that gives without taking runs
out of tension around chapter 40 and never recovers it, no matter how much happens.

---

## The cost rule

Every chapter takes something. The scale varies; zero is not on the scale.

| scale | examples |
|---|---|
| **small** (most chapters) | time, a lie told, an option closed, an ally's patience, a favour spent, information given away |
| **medium** (every ~5) | a plan fails for a reason the MC could not have prevented; a relationship changes shape; a resource is gone |
| **large** (every arc) | a person, a capability, a belief, a place they cannot return to |
| **defining** (every 2–3 arcs) | a loss that is partly the MC's own fault and cannot be repaired |

Record every cost in the plan row and in the CCS block. Log the MC's in the pain ledger
(`state/growth.md`).

**Legibility test.** State the cost in one concrete sentence. *"He suffered"* fails.
*"He can't go back to Ashfall, and his sister is still there"* passes.

## Escalation is about what is lost, not what is fought

The corpus escalates by enemy power level: stronger opponent, higher realm, bigger number. That
curve is unwinnable — it forces power creep, and after four rounds the reader has stopped
tracking magnitudes.

Escalate the **stake** instead:

```
1  the MC's own safety                     (cheap; readers assume plot armour)
2  something the MC owns or has built
3  someone the MC is responsible for
4  someone the MC loves
5  something the MC believes about themselves
6  something other people need that only the MC can protect
```

A rung-5 threat with a weak opponent beats a rung-1 threat with a god. Design each arc's ladder
along this axis, and let opponent strength follow the story rather than lead it.

## Sources of conflict — use all four

Relying on external violence alone is why long serials go flat.

| source | question | example |
|---|---|---|
| **External** | who is stopping them | the Guild wants the ledger destroyed |
| **Interpersonal** | who wants a different outcome and is not an enemy | her brother wants to take the deal |
| **Internal** | what in them fights the goal | she cannot ask for help |
| **Situational** | what the world imposes regardless of will | winter, debt, an illness, a law |

At least two per chapter. The interpersonal and internal ones are what make the external ones
matter.

## The antagonist standard

- Wants something legible and defensible from their own seat. Write it in two sentences.
- Right about something the MC is wrong about.
- Competent, demonstrated before they lose anything.
- Paying a cost for their position.
- Acts on their own schedule — **an antagonist who only reacts to the MC is scenery.** Keep their
  offscreen clock in `state/timeline.md`.

An arc's antagonist must reach or exceed the MC's `intel_tier` at least once (see
`mc-intel-meter`).

## Failure design

Serials need real failures, and low-effort drafting avoids them. Schedule them.

Rules for a failure that works:

1. **Caused by a choice**, not by bad luck. Luck can worsen a failure, never cause it.
2. **Traceable to a character flaw or a genuine information gap**, both established earlier.
3. **Consequences persist.** A failure undone in the next chapter did not happen.
4. **The MC's competence is intact.** They failed for reasons, not by being written stupid. See
   `mc-intel-meter`'s three legal levers.
5. **The reader can see the road not taken.** Failure that had no alternative is misfortune, not
   drama.

## Victory design

Wins are necessary — this is not a misery engine, and readers of this format came for progress.
The requirement is that wins be **paid for**.

- Set up the means of victory *before* it is needed, so it reads as earned rather than granted.
- Attach a price: exposure, a debt, an ally's trust, a rule broken, a capability spent.
- Let the win create the next problem. This is the arc-to-arc engine.
- Occasionally let the MC win something they didn't want, or find that winning cost more than
  losing would have.

**No deus ex machina.** New capabilities, allies and resources arrive *before* the chapter that
needs them, and preferably before the reader knows they are needed.

## Tension maintenance across a serial

- **Never resolve every thread at once.** Payoffs are staggered so at least two remain open.
- **Answer questions with questions.** A reveal that closes a mystery should open a smaller,
  sharper one.
- **Standing offscreen clocks.** Two or three things advancing regardless of the MC, logged in
  `plan/timeline.md` with a firing chapter. Dread comes from clocks. `timeline-engine` runs them,
  along with how each driver reacts to the MC and how fast.
- **A cool stretch is not an idle stretch.** After a climax, lower the temperature but keep the
  goal, the turn and the cost. Chapters with no want are where readers leave.

## Diagnostic — a stretch feels flat

Ask, in order:

1. Have the last five chapters cost the MC anything? (Check the pain ledger.)
2. Is anything currently threatened at rung 3+?
3. Does the antagonist have a plan running that the MC does not know about?
4. Is there an internal conflict on the page, or only external ones?
5. Has the MC wanted something specific in each of the last five chapters?
6. Has anything the MC won created a new problem?

The first "no" is the fault.
