# conflict-engine — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 1**, every chapter. Written here rather than summarised there,
because the cost is the gate: if it is empty the chapter is not ready, and no amount of drafting
fixes a chapter that was never going to take anything.

**Produce:** this chapter's cost in one concrete sentence, its stake rung, the two active conflict
sources, and what the antagonist did for their own reasons.

## The cost — never zero

| scale | examples |
|---|---|
| **small** (most chapters) | time, a lie told, an option closed, an ally's patience, information given away |
| **medium** (every ~5) | a plan fails for a reason the MC could not have prevented; a relationship changes shape |
| **large** (every arc) | a person, a capability, a belief, a place they cannot return to |
| **defining** (every 2–3 arcs) | a loss partly the MC's own fault, and unrepairable |

**Legibility test.** *"He suffered"* fails. *"He can't go back to Ashfall, and his sister is still
there"* passes.

## The stake rung — escalate this, not enemy power

```
1 the MC's own safety      4 someone the MC loves
2 something they built     5 something they believe about themselves
3 someone they protect     6 something only they can protect
```

A rung-5 threat with a weak opponent beats a rung-1 threat with a god. The rung and the *gap*
(`power-scaling`'s pressure) are two axes: raising both every arc is the treadmill. An arc that
raises the rung while holding the tier still is usually the better arc.

A stake only counts if the reader can price it — in the opening arc the ceiling is
`story-opening` §3, not this ladder.

## Two of these four, minimum

**External** (who is stopping them) · **Interpersonal** (who wants a different outcome and is not
an enemy) · **Internal** (what in them fights the goal) · **Situational** (what the world imposes).
The interpersonal and internal ones are what make the external ones matter.

## The antagonist, this chapter

Acting on their own schedule — **an antagonist who only reacts to the MC is scenery.** They want
something defensible from their own seat, and they are right about something the MC is wrong about.

If this chapter contains a win, it was set up earlier and it is priced. If it contains a failure,
a choice caused it and the consequence persists: `conflict-engine` §Failure design.
