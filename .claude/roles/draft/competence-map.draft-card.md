# competence-map — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 1**, every chapter. Written here rather than summarised there,
because who has to ask is a *plot* decision made before drafting — and once a scene is written with
a convenient mouth answering, the fix is a rewrite, not an edit.

**One card, two owners.** Who has to ask is `competence-map`'s; the **cast tier** of anyone new is
`character-profile`'s, which owns the tiers, the debut and the walk-on roster. They are the same
decision seen from two sides — who is in this scene, and what can each of them actually supply — and
a walk-on's third stroke *is* their competence map. Each half names its owner; the card budget is
why they share a file (`docs/creative-latitude.md`).

**Produce two lines:** the thing this chapter needs known, whose map covers it, and who must go to
somebody else for it — and the cast tier of anyone new, decided **before they speak**.

## The rule the skill rests on

**An unlisted domain is `none`, not "probably fine."** The instinct is to treat an unlisted domain
as adequate because adequacy keeps the scene moving. If this chapter needs a character to know
something not on their map, either they do not know it — *which is the scene* — or they learn it on
the page and it goes on the map.

Deep expertise budget: MC at most 3 domains above `professional`, other tier A at most 2, tier B at
most 1, tier C exactly 1 and it is their job.

## Pick the kind of not-knowing

"They don't know" is a wall. These are doors:

| kind | produces |
|---|---|
| **ignorant** | does not know the word; someone must explain, or they proceed blind |
| **wrong** | **the most useful.** Confident, outdated or folk model — they act competently on a false premise |
| **shallow** | knows the jargon, fails the second question |
| **rusty** | knew it once, in another era; the half that is wrong is dangerous |

**Prefer wrong to ignorant.** A character who does not know does nothing; a character who is wrong
does something, and the story is made of what people do.

## The referral

A specialist's most useful knowledge is often **who to ask**, and the asking costs — a favour owed,
a fee, an admission of ignorance to someone who will remember it. Delegation reads as *more*
capable, not less. Write the referral instead of an improvised second specialty.

Somebody in this chapter says they do not know, asks the person who does, or is visibly wrong.

## While drafting

Every stated fact carries a provenance: **taught · did · told · read · openly guessing.** If you
cannot name one, they cannot say the line. Foreknowledge is a sixth, bounded to the declared grain
and inventory — and it is never provenance for a *skill*.

Somebody learning something across chapters: `references/acquisition-ladder.md`. A god, immortal or
artificial mind with `knowledge_scope: broad`: `references/broad-knowledge.md`.

## Anyone new — `character-profile`'s half

**Decide the tier before they speak**, and start everyone at **C** — a promotion is earned by a
third appearance or by changing the plot, never granted because a scene felt important. A walk-on
who acquires a psychology mid-draft is how a long serial runs out of context
(`character-profile` owns the tiers).

| tier | promote when | record |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | the story is about their beliefs | full file |
| **B** | recurs across an arc, does a job, has no arc | short file |
| **C** | one to three scenes and gone | one roster line |
| **D** | says *halt* | nothing |

For a **C**, invent three strokes on the spot: something they want to be finished with in the next
five minutes, one repeatable habit, and — the stroke that does the work — **one fact about how the
world is going for them**, a levy, a shortage, a grudge, something that changed at their work last
month. That third stroke is also the whole of their competence: they know their own job and nothing
behind it. Plus one axis off default from `voice-separation`'s matrix, or they speak in the
narrator's register, which is the MC's.

Do not stop to build a profile mid-draft; sketch, log the roster line in Step 5, promote at a third
appearance. The tiers, the swap test, the doomed-extra rule and what each record contains are
`character-profile`'s — `character-profile/SKILL.md` and `character-profile/references/tier-a-and-b.md`.

`bias-guard` applies at every tier and is broken most often at C. One detail is all a walk-on
gets, and the corpus default will make that detail a stereotype unless the stroke is chosen
deliberately.
