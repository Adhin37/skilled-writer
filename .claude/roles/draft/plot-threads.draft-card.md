# plot-threads — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 1**, every chapter. Written here rather than summarised there,
because thread ops are decided against the whole open ledger, and a chapter drafted first and
reconciled afterwards is how promises get quietly abandoned.

**One card, two owners.** The ops are `plot-threads`'; the **offstage question** is
`timeline-engine`'s, which owns the world clock, the divergence ledger and the ending contract.
Both answer the same question from opposite ends — what is moving that this chapter has to respect,
whether or not the MC can see it — so they are one card, and each half names its owner. The card
budget is why they share a file (`docs/creative-latitude.md`).

**Produce two lines:** the thread ops firing this chapter, in CCS notation, for the `thr>` line;
and the offstage question, one line, for the `wld>` line.

| op | notation | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| open | `~T14` | the promise is made — noticeable without being announced |
| advance | `^T03` | new information, higher stakes, or a step closer. Does not resolve |
| pay | `vT09` | the promise is kept |
| abandon | `xT21` | deliberate retirement, with a written reason and usually an on-page acknowledgement |

The read-set carries the open threads with their ages. Decide against it, not from memory.

## What the ledger wants from this chapter

- **5–9 open.** Below 4 the story reads as episodic; above 12 nothing feels urgent.
- **All three tiers alive** — 1–2 novel-level, 2–3 arc-level, 3–4 chapter-level. The chapter-level
  tier is what makes a serial feel like it is moving; if readers say it drags, that tier is empty.
- **Anything past its `due`** gets a reason in `carried` and a new `due`, in this pass. A thread
  carried twice is escalated on the page or abandoned on the page.
- **A payoff here** was planted at least twice, earlier, and does not explain its own foreshadowing.

**What counts as payment:** the promise the reader was made, answered. A mystery paid by revealing
another mystery has been renamed, not paid.

## Opening arc only

Four promise-ledger threads are due earlier than anything else in the book — the world anchor, the
genre contract, `opening.promise` touched on the page, and the advantage's first legible win. They
are promises to a reader who has not yet decided to keep reading, so they cannot be paid late:
`plot-threads` §The promise ledger — arc 1 only.

Planting a setup, or auditing for forgotten promises: `references/foreshadowing.md`. A reversal
the reader is meant to re-read their way into: `references/reveals-and-reversals.md`.

## The offstage question — `timeline-engine`'s half

**One line, for the `wld>` field: which piece of the world moved out of the MC's sight this
chapter?** It is why a reader believes the world is running at all, and it is the first thing dropped
when a chapter is drafted from the MC outward. Most chapters it is small — and small because the
MC was not there, not because nothing happened.

Pitch it to `timeline.reactivity` in the read-set CONFIG: at 1 the world moves only in its own
scenes, at 3 the opposition notices and re-plans, at 5 it reorganises around the MC.

Two things this card will not let you skip:

- **If the MC touched the world track**, it gets a divergence row, and the second-order effect is
  on the page rather than owed.
- **Before firing any world-track event, the point-of-no-return check** — does this make
  `ending.contract` unreachable? If yes, change its scale, timing or target. The world may cost
  the MC anything; it may never close the road, and `ending.non_negotiables` is absolute.

The dial, the ledger, the butterfly budget and the arc-level obligation are `timeline-engine`'s —
`timeline-engine/SKILL.md`, with `timeline-engine/references/reaction-and-governor.md` when a faction is about to
move and `timeline-engine/references/fanfic-mode.md` where canon is the world track.
