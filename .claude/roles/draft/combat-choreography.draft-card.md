# combat-choreography — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` Phase A when the module is on **and this chapter contains physical
conflict**. If it does not, close the card in one line.

A fight is a scene and takes the same four parts from `scene-craft`. This card decides the five
things that are specific to violence, and writing a fight without them produces the
exchange-by-exchange drift readers skim.

**Produce five lines for the brief:**

```
stake     if she loses, the boy is taken - she survives either way and both of them know it
ends      she wins, and breaks the hand she needs for next week's work
turn      he stops trying to win and starts trying to delay - reinforcements are coming
terrain   the wet ramp, the stacked crates, the door that only opens inward
asymmetry he has reach and patience; she knows the building
```

## The five

1. **Stake beyond survival.** The reader assumes the MC lives. Make the fight about something
   they can actually lose: someone else, a secret, a position, a principle.
2. **How it ends, and what winning costs.** Decided now, not discovered mid-draft.
3. **The turn** — the moment it becomes a different fight than it started as.
4. **Terrain** — three concrete features that will be *used*. Terrain is what makes a fight this
   fight rather than any fight.
5. **The asymmetry** — what each side is better at. Two people good at the same thing is a coin
   flip and reads as one.

## While drafting

- **Compress, compress, expand.** Routine exchanges go in a clause; the decisive beat gets the
  room. Do not narrate every strike.
- **Intention over technique.** What someone is *trying to do* is legible; the name of the move
  is not.
- **Re-anchor at every turn.** The reader always knows where the bodies are and what is between
  them.
- **Damage accumulates inside the scene.** A knee to the ribs in the first exchange makes a
  different fighter for the rest of it.
- **One idea per beat** — not a technique, a taunt, a sensory detail and a thought in one
  paragraph.
- Fear, adrenaline and the shake afterwards are more evocative than any strike description.

Power use pays in the same scene (`power-system`). A battle rather than a fight — units, a field
the POV cannot see, supply — is `battle-scale`, a different craft and a different module.
