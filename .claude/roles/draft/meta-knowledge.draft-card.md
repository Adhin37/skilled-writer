# meta-knowledge — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 1**, but **only when `mc.foreknowledge` is set**. Skip it entirely
otherwise. Written here rather than summarised there, because a spend is decided before drafting —
what it costs and what it invalidates are part of the chapter's shape, not bookkeeping afterwards.

**Produce:** what the MC knows that bears on today, at what grain, what using it costs, and what it
invalidates.

## The grain is a hard ceiling on every plan

| grain | can | cannot |
|---|---|---|
| `episode-precise` | name events, their order, roughly when | know what happened offstage in the source |
| `major-beats` | name the big turns and who is at them | order them confidently, or date them |
| `impressions` | recognise a name, feel dread at a place, know a person is dangerous | state what happens, or when |
| `fandom-corrupted` | recall vividly and in detail — **and be wrong** | separate what they read from what they were told about it |

> **An `impressions` MC does not produce a date. A `major-beats` MC does not produce an order.** If
> a plan on the page needs precision the grain does not license, the plan is a cheat, and a reader
> who knows the source feels it before they can name it.

## Three axes, held apart

Foreknowledge is **not** intelligence (`mc-intel-meter` owns the tier — a tier-2 MC with perfect
foreknowledge still misuses good information) and **not** competence — `competence-map` owns the
acquisition ladder and the rule that foreknowledge is no provenance for a skill.

Every fact stated from it carries a provenance — read it, was told it, saw it, or openly guessing.

## Ordering, and the observer paradox

`foreknowledge_first_win_ch` **<** `foreknowledge_fails_ch`. The win comes first, it is **legible**
(a reader can point at it and see foreknowledge caused it; somebody is surprised by an outcome the
MC was not) and **load-bearing**. An arc whose every rung is a foreknowledge failure is not a ladder,
it is a slide.

> **Acting on foreknowledge is the fastest way to destroy it.** The future the MC remembers is the
> future in which they did not act.

So a plot-changing spend here moves at least one *other* inventory row toward `invalidated`. Big
spends buy big outcomes and cost big swathes of the ledger — and a smart MC works this out and
starts **rationing**, which is a far more interesting character than one who is merely wrong a lot.

**What does not decay** is knowledge of people's natures — what someone wants, fears, will do under
pressure. Events butterfly; character does not. That is the graceful path out of the oracle problem.
