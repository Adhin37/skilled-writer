# grimdark-consequences — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` Phase A when the module is on. The promise this module makes to the
reader is that **what happens, stays happened** — and the drafting decision is what that costs
today.

**Produce two lines for the brief:**

```
takes    Ivar's right hand. It does not come back, and he was the one who could write
keeps    the boy is still alive, and the reader is now afraid for him
```

## Decision 1 — what this chapter takes, and whether it stays taken

- Injuries persist. Recovery runs in chapters, not paragraphs, and some things do not return. A
  limp mentioned once and forgotten is worse than no limp — log it in continuity facts now.
- Victories are partial. Name the shortfall before drafting: the ones not saved, the thing not
  recovered, the price that was not worth it.
- Moral costs stay paid. Somebody who did something unforgivable is not forgiven by the
  narrative — it reaches their voice delta (`character-development`) and how others treat them.
- **No retroactive rescue, ever.** Nobody was secretly alive; the sacrifice was not unnecessary.
  This voids reader trust faster than anything else available, and it cannot be re-earned.

## Decision 2 — what is left worth protecting

The harder half, and the one that keeps the book readable. Unrelieved bleakness is as monotonous
as unrelieved triumph and loses readers for the same reason: **nothing is at risk when everything
is already lost.**

Name, for this chapter, one thing the reader wants protected. A small mercy that costs the giver
something and holds is what makes the losses land.

## While drafting

- The chapter still has a goal and a turn. Suffering is not a plot; a person choosing under
  pressure is.
- **Grief is specific and brief.** One paragraph of the wrong practical detail beats three pages
  of mourning.
- Competence still improves outcomes, or the reader disengages from every decision the MC makes.
- Everybody who dies wanted something. Nobody exists to be killed for effect.
- Suffering as spectacle, and sexual violence in particular, stays off the page; its consequences
  belong to the person it happened to (`bias-guard`).

This module raises the severity band `conflict-engine` owns — check its ladder rather than
guessing at the size of today's cost.
