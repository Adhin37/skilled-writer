# scene-craft — draft card

Opened by `write-chapter` **Step 1**, every chapter. Written here rather than summarised there,
because a chapter's shape is decided before a word of it exists, and a chapter drafted without
these five answers is the one that reads as "things happened".

**Produce:** the scene count, one goal/obstacle/turn/exit row per scene, and the `delivers:` line.

## Per scene

| element | the answer must be |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | concrete, pursued in this scene — not a mood |
| **Stakes** | knowable to the reader *now*, not later |
| **Obstacle** | someone or something with its own reason |
| **Turn** | the thing that is not what they expected |
| **Exit** | `Yes, but` (workhorse) · `No, and` (escalator) · `No, but` (after a hard blow) · `Yes, and` (rare) |

**Never a plain yes.** A scene the POV character simply wins is a scene the reader skims.

Default shape: cold open ~7% → scene 1 ~40% → follow-through ~8% → `* * *` → scene 2 ~40% (the
change lands here) → hook ~5%. One scene suits sustained pressure — an interrogation, a duel;
three suit converging lines. Proportions, not word counts.

## The chapter, before drafting

| | the question | not ready when |
|---|---|---|
| **Want** | what the POV character is after | the answer is "to react to things" |
| **Friction** | the person, rule or scarcity in the way | the only obstacle is the MC's hesitation, twice running |
| **Change** | what is materially different at the end | the exit state equals the entry state |
| **Cost** | what was paid, and by whom | nothing — go to `conflict-engine` |
| **Next** | what the reader now needs to see | the hook asks what the chapter already answered |

**Change is load-bearing, and it is not a summary of events.** *"He asks about the recount"* is
events; *"he is now someone the matron watches on purpose"* is a change. If you cannot state it
without narrating the chapter, the chapter is not ready to draft. This answer becomes `delivers:`.

Any repeated beat — a second refusal, a second failed attempt — costs more than the first, or one
of the two gets cut.
