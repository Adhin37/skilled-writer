# social-perception — the observation ladder

Open this when somebody in the chapter is judging somebody, and the question is what they are
allowed to come away with.

---

## The ladder

Every read climbs the same four rungs. The tier decides how far up a character gets, and — more
usefully — **where they stop and commit.**

| rung | what it is | example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 **Signal** | the observable fact | she is still holding the cup |
| 2 **Anomaly** | the fact is out of place | she has not drunk from it since he sat down |
| 3 **Reading** | a cause proposed for the anomaly | she is waiting for him to leave first |
| 4 **Commitment** | acting on the reading as though it were settled | she asks the question she was saving |

- **Tier 1** stops at rung 1 and does not notice there was an anomaly.
- **Tier 2** reaches rung 2 and jumps to a rung-3 reading about **themselves**: *she is angry with
  me.*
- **Tier 3** reaches rung 3 competently and goes straight to rung 4 without revisiting. This is
  the most common cast tier and the most useful, because the failure is built in.
- **Tier 4** reaches rung 3, holds two readings at once, and tests one. They commit late.
- **Tier 5** reaches a rung-3 reading the subject has not reached about themselves — and is at
  rung 1 about their own life.

The useful writing question is never "what does she work out". It is **where does she stop, and
what does she do from there.**

## What a tier may put on the page

A limited viewpoint may report rungs 1 and 2 as fact. Rung 3 is always hedged, always the
character's, never the narrator's:

| allowed | not allowed |
|---|---|
| She had not drunk from the cup since he sat down | She was uncomfortable |
| Whatever he had come to say, he had decided it before he arrived | He had decided to refuse her |
| The captain's men were watching the door, not the stage | The captain's men were nervous |

The right-hand column is not weaker writing, it is a different POV. And the left column is doing
more work: the reader gets the evidence and reaches the conclusion themselves, which is the only
way they ever feel clever.

**Free indirect discourse does most of the hedging for free.** *Whatever he had come to say, he
had decided it before he arrived* is the character's inference in the character's rhythm, with no
*she thought that* anywhere near it (`narrator-voice`).

## Writing a misread so the reader can see past it

The reader needs to be able to reconstruct the true cause afterwards without having been told it
at the time. Three moves, and the second is the one that gets skipped.

1. **Put the true cause in the scene, doing another job.** The cup is mentioned because she is
   holding it while she talks about the rota. It is characterisation on the first read and
   evidence on the second — the same disguise-by-function a clue gets (`mystery-clues` owns that
   technique where the novel has the module on; it works identically here).

2. **Let a second character register something different.** Not an explanation — a reaction that
   does not fit the POV character's reading. Somebody else looks at the cup. The reader notices
   the mismatch without being able to name it, which is exactly the state you want them in.

3. **Do not correct it in the narration.** No *he could not have known*, no *he would think about
   that moment for a long time afterwards*. The correction arrives as a consequence, later, in a
   scene — never as a narrator's aside (`prose-quality`, and the ban on the knowing narrator).

## Emotional signatures — building the four

Per tier-A and tier-B character, and they go in the profile. Two rules make them work.

**Source them from the life, not from the emotion.** Ask what this person did for years and what
it trained into them, then let the feeling come out through that. A clerk's fear looks like
paperwork. A soldier's grief looks like maintenance. A person raised in a loud house gets quiet;
a person raised in a silent one gets loud.

**Make one of them a false friend.** At least one signature should be readable as a different
feeling by somebody who does not know them well — that is the engine of §4's misreads, and it
costs nothing to install:

```
Berrow
  fear      precise. Sorts what is already sorted. Reads as competence, and gets him promoted
  anger     silence, then a question with a date in it
  grief     eats properly for the first time in weeks. Nobody has ever understood this
  relief    argues about something small and unrelated
```

Notice that three of those four could be mistaken for something else by a stranger, and that the
one which cannot — the question with a date in it — is the one his intimates watch for.

## Non-human and ambiguous bodies

Where a character is not human, the signature is still four entries and still legible from
outside; what changes is that **rung 3 gets harder for everyone**, because the observer has no
priors. That is a resource rather than a problem: a reader watching a person misread a
non-human's stillness is watching the misread mechanism at its clearest.
`character-profile/references/nonhuman.md` owns how such a character is built; this file owns
what somebody looking at them is allowed to conclude.
