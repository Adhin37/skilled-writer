# social-fabric — belief, ritual and taboo

Open this when the story enters a temple, a funeral, a wedding, an oath, a festival or a taboo —
or when question 5 of the six is answered and the answer has not reached a single scene.

Belief is the cheapest authenticity available and the most commonly skipped. It costs three
sentences of design and it supplies oaths that mean something, a calendar, a reason strangers
trust each other, and a way for a character to be shunned without a law being broken.

**This is not about whether the gods are real.** In a fantasy setting they may be, and that is
`power-system`'s question. This is about what *people do*, which is the same problem either way —
and is frequently funnier and stranger when the gods demonstrably exist.

---

## The four questions

One or two sentences each, in `bible/society.md` under question 5.

| # | question | what it buys |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What happens when you die**, according to most people here? | every funeral, every risk anyone takes, every threat that is not about pain |
| 2 | **What do you do on the days that matter** — birth, marriage, death, harvest, war? | the scenes that show a community rather than a crowd |
| 3 | **What is forbidden**, and what happens to somebody who does it anyway? | a second enforcement system beside the law, and it reaches where law does not |
| 4 | **Who speaks for it**, and what do they get out of that? | an institution with interests, which is an antagonist or an ally with a legible want |

## Oaths — the most useful of these by a distance

An oath is a **promise with a supernatural or social enforcement**, and it does something no
contract can: it binds a character the reader believes will not break it, which means the plot can
be built on it holding.

Design one, at least: the words, what invokes it, who witnesses, and **what the breaker loses**.
Then the story has a device where a character can be trapped by their own integrity rather than by
a locked door — the strongest obstacle available, because the reader cannot wish it away.

The counter-move is equally good: somebody who takes the oath knowing they will break it, and what
that costs them afterwards in a world where everyone else believes.

## Ritual as scene structure

A ritual is a scene with the goal, sequence and stakes **already supplied**, which is why they are
such reliable chapters. The reader learns what is supposed to happen in the first thirty seconds,
and from then on every deviation is legible without explanation. Somebody arriving late, somebody
not saying the words, the wrong person holding the thing — the reader reads all of it correctly
with no narration at all.

Two rules:

- **Show it working once before you break it.** A ritual the reader has never seen performed
  correctly cannot be disrupted meaningfully (`story-craft` owns the build-up principle).
- **Somebody present does not believe** and is going through with it anyway. That character is
  where the scene's tension lives, and they are almost always more interesting than the believer.

## Unevenness, applied to belief

The §3 rule binds here hardest, because invented religions default to uniform:

- **The official account and the street account differ**, and both are held by people who would
  say they believe the same thing.
- **Practice outruns belief.** Most people do the thing without holding the doctrine — they do it
  because their mother did, because it is what you do, because it would feel wrong not to.
- **Somebody is making money.** Temples have budgets, festivals have vendors, relics have a market.
- **There is a heresy, and it has a point.** A dissenting reading that is defensible gives you
  conflict between two decent people, which is worth more than any villain.
- **What the powerful believe is not what the poor believe**, and the powerful know the poor
  version and find it useful.

## Taboo

The sharpest tool in this file, because it operates where law does not.

A taboo is a rule **nobody will explain**, enforced by shunning rather than by punishment. A
character who breaks one learns they have by the room going quiet — and the reader learns the
shape of the society in a single beat, with no exposition, which is exactly what `world-texture`
wants from a world fact.

Give the novel two: one the MC knows and obeys without thinking, and one they do not know yet.

## Where this must not go

`bias-guard` governs this file absolutely and overrides everything above. An invented religion is
not a real one with the names changed; a practice is never treated by the narration as evidence of
what its people inherently are; and the believers are not uniform, credulous, or a mass. Where a
faith in the story does harm, somebody inside it is resisting, and they are not the only decent
person in it.
