# character-development — harm that does not heal

Open this when something taken in an earlier arc has not come back and the character is still
carrying it, or when you are about to write a recovery the story never paid for.

`conflict-engine/references/aftermath.md` owns the cost while it is still being paid — the scene,
and the days after. This file is about what is left when the days run out and the character is
simply **different now**.

---

## The third thing

The ladder in `ladders.md` has two motions and most drafts try to force everything into one of
them. Harm is neither.

| | what moves | does it revert | where it is recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| **rung advance** | a belief about themselves or the world | only by regression, which is a scene | `rung`, `voice delta so far` |
| **regression** | their position on the ladder, under pressure | **yes** — temporary and visible by rule | `rung 4 (regressed from 4→3, ch 118)` |
| **lasting harm** | the baseline the ladder runs on | **no** | the `lasting harm` column, read every chapter |

A character can advance a rung *and* be permanently worse off, in the same arc, from the same
event. That combination is most of what readers mean by a story feeling grown-up, and a toolkit
with only two motions cannot express it.

## What counts

Three forms, in ascending order of usefulness.

1. **A capability that now costs more.** Not gone — dearer. She can still climb; it takes her
   twice as long and she will not do it where anyone can see. *Gone* is a different thing and
   belongs in the character's continuity facts (`character-profile`), or on the ladder if a tier
   moved (`power-scaling`).
2. **An avoidance.** A thing they route around. The routing is visible on the page well before the
   reason is, and the gap between those two is free tension — nobody has to explain anything.
3. **A new regression condition.** The most useful, because it plugs into machinery that already
   exists: every tier-A profile already lists what slides a character backwards. Harm adds one to
   the list, permanently, and now an old event has teeth in chapters that are not about it.

**What does not count:** a mood, a bad memory, or a wound that is still healing. The first two are
not structural and the third is `conflict-engine`'s until it stops improving.

## How it reaches the page

The failure here is the one this repo keeps rediscovering: a drafter who never mentions the harm
never contradicts the ledger, and writes damage that costs nothing. So the requirement is
positive rather than prohibitive.

> **Harm that is never inconvenient is not harm.** It reaches the reader as a choice made
> differently, never as narration about the past.

The author's job is not to mention it. It is to **schedule the scene that asks for the thing they
can no longer do cheaply** — and then let them pay, or refuse, or send somebody else. One such
scene per arc is enough to keep every earlier cost live.

Three deliveries that work, roughly in order of how often they should be used:

- **The refusal nobody comments on.** They volunteer for the other job. Somebody who knows why
  says nothing, and that silence is the scene.
- **The workaround.** They have built a method around the gap, and it is visibly worse and
  visibly practised. Competence at avoidance is more affecting than the injury was.
- **Someone else noticing first.** Harm the character will not name, named by a person with less
  standing to name it.

## Healing, and what it costs

This is not a licence to damage the whole cast. A book in which everyone is permanently marked has
the same flat register as a book in which nobody is, and it retires the contrast that makes any
single case land — keep somebody unharmed as the measuring stick, the way a rate-1 character who
does not change is the measuring stick in `ladders.md`.

Harm may resolve. The rules are the same as a rung's:

- **Resolution is a scene with a price**, logged in `growth.md` with the chapter it happened in.
- **It is never undone by elapsed time or a time skip.** Recovery reported across a gap is the
  campaign-clause defect, and `story-craft` owns it.
- **It leaves the regression condition behind.** Rung 5 is *changed, at a price, with the old
  belief still audible when they are tired*, and resolved harm works the same way.

## Where this is not the right file

| the case | the owner |
|---|---|
| the cost in the scene, and over the days after | `conflict-engine/references/aftermath.md` |
| severity bands, and what may never be undone at all | `grimdark-consequences` — the optional module. This file is the always-on version, because even a hopeful novel teaches the reader to discount a cost that evaporates |
| what fear, grief or anger look like on *this* person | `social-perception` owns the emotional signature, and it should already be written down. Harm changes a signature; it never replaces one |
| a skill stage that genuinely dropped | `competence-map` owns the acquisition ladder, in both directions |

## Anti-patterns

| pattern | why it fails | instead |
|---|---|---|
| Harm that surfaces only as intrusive memory | flashback is the one delivery every reader has seen, and it stops the scene to do it | let it surface as a decision, in a scene that is about something else |
| Harm as a personality | the character stops wanting things, and a character who wants nothing cannot carry a scene | they want exactly what they wanted before. Harm shapes *how* they go after it |
| The scheduled breakdown | it arrives on the chapter the plan says, which is the one place the reader is braced for it | let it arrive inconveniently, over something too small, in front of the wrong person |
| Cured by being loved | hands a person's arc to somebody else's presence | `lead-interest` may be why they try. The price is still theirs to pay |
| The whole cast marked | contrast dies and the register flattens | one unharmed person makes every other case legible |
| Harm nobody else can see | if only the narrator knows, it is not in the story | somebody notices, and is wrong about why |

## Recording it

The `lasting harm` column in the arc-ladder table of `state/growth.md`: one clause, in the form
**what they can no longer do cheaply**, with the chapter it happened in. `—` is a legitimate and
common entry.

It is read every chapter, because it arrives in the read-set with the rest of the growth row and
costs nothing to carry.

**At every arc boundary**, run it against the three largest costs of the arc — the test in
`conflict-engine/references/aftermath.md` §The rule. A cost that produced no lasting harm and no
rung movement was a scene ending, not a price.
