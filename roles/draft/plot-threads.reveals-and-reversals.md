# plot-threads — reveals and reversals

Open this when a chapter turns something the reader believed, and the novel does not have
`optional.mystery-clues` on. That module owns fair-play mechanics — the clue ledger, red herrings,
the knowledge modes — for stories whose engine is a question. This file is the general case: every
serial reverses things, and the discipline is the same even when nobody is investigating anything.

---

## What a reversal actually is

Not new information. **A re-reading of information the reader already has.**

> A twist works when the reader's first reading was *complete* — nothing was missing, everything
> parsed — and the second reading is also complete, and better.

That is the whole craft. If the first reading had a hole in it, the reader was waiting rather than
surprised. If the second reading requires a fact they never had, it is not a twist, it is an
announcement.

So the unit of work is not the reveal. It is the **double-valid scene**: an exchange that reads
correctly and satisfyingly under two interpretations, written to be good under the first one. A
scene that exists only to be re-read later is a scene that reads as odd the first time, and
readers notice oddness immediately.

## The three plants, and why the second one matters most

`references/foreshadowing.md` owns the plant ladder. Applied to a reversal:

1. **Ambient** — the fact arrives doing another job entirely. Nobody weights it.
2. **Noticed and dismissed** — somebody in the story remarks on it and is given a reason not to
   care. This is the one drafts skip, and it is what converts a fair plant into a *satisfying*
   one, because the reader remembers having been offered it.
3. **Loaded and unexplained** — close enough to the reveal that the reader feels the floor move.

## What the reveal has to cost

A reversal that only changes the reader's understanding is trivia. It has to change somebody's
position:

- a **decision** is now unavoidable, and unwelcome;
- a **relationship** is re-priced — somebody has to decide what to do about what they now know;
- an **earlier cost turns out to have been paid for nothing**, or for something worse;
- a **thread closes and a sharper one opens**. Close large, open small.

If none of those is true, the reveal belongs in a later chapter where one of them can be.

## Reversal types that earn a serial's trust

| type | the mechanism | fails when |
|---|---|---|
| **Allegiance** | somebody's loyalty was to a different thing all along | the reader never saw them act on the real loyalty |
| **Cause** | the event happened, but not for the reason everyone assumed | the true cause was never on the page |
| **Scale** | the problem is smaller or larger than it looked | it is only larger, every time, which is escalation not reversal |
| **Agency** | the person the MC was reacting to was themselves reacting to somebody | the third party arrives new |
| **Self** | the MC was wrong about their own motive | the narration had already told the reader the true one |

## The three that do not

- **The unearned resurrection.** Nobody was secretly alive. This is the fastest way to void a
  reader's trust, and it does not recover.
- **The retroactive rule.** A power, a law or a relationship that turns out to have always worked
  differently. The world may have surprises; it may not have had different rules.
- **The narrator's lie.** A limited POV may be wrong about anything. The prose may not state
  something false. Misinterpretation and omission are fair; assertion is not — and this holds in
  every genre, not only in a mystery.

## Ageing, which is this file's real subject

A reversal is a promise, and it ages like every other thread. Once the reader can feel that
something is being withheld, the withholding is on the clock: **the tension of an unanswered
question decays**, and past a certain point the reader stops believing there is an answer.

So a planted reversal gets a `due` in `state/threads.md` like anything else, and the standing rule
applies — past its horizon it is escalated, paid, or deferred **with a stated reason**. Perpetual
deferral is what readers mean when they say a serial stopped going anywhere, and a twist held back
too long lands as an admission rather than a surprise.
