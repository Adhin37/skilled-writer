# dialogue-voice — same side

Open this when two people in the scene want the same thing and the exchange is going flat, or when
every conversation in the chapter so far has been a parry.

An exchange between people who are not fighting is not a scene with the conflict removed. It is a
different mechanism, and it does more work per line than an argument does, because agreement is
where people stop guarding their voice. Benchmark run #5 put the identical parry in three
different mouths across five chapters, because nothing described any other way for a conversation
to go.

## Four moves, none of which is "they are nice to each other"

| move | what it is | why it separates voices |
|---|---|---|
| **Shorthand** | they skip the part they both already know | what a pair omits is specific to that pair. Nobody else in the book can have this exchange |
| **Interruption as agreement** | finishing the thought, overlapping, answering before the question lands | the opposite of the parry, and it uses the same punctuation (`dialogue-voice` §Rules, interruption and overlap) |
| **The joke that includes them** | teasing that would be cruel from anyone else | `voice-separation` owns the `wit` axis and caps who has it — this move belongs to whoever already carries `warm` there |
| **Unguarded competence** | one of them says the true thing first, with no angle on it | the strong short lines in `dialogue-voice` §Development and voice are strongest between allies |

## It still obeys everything else

The transplant test (`voice-separation`) does not relax: if either line could move to another
mouth unedited, the rapport is generic. Both speakers still want something — they just want the
same thing, and the friction is with the world rather than with each other.

**The failure to watch for is blandness, not frictionlessness.** A warm exchange in which both
people speak the same pleasant, unplaced register is the thing to fix, and the fix is the eight
fingerprint fields, not an argument. A reader who cannot tell who is talking has lost the scene
whatever its temperature.

Rationed by `tone.warmth` (`narrator-voice` §The tone axis). At `cold`, this file is off.
