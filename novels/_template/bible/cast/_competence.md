# Cast competence map

Maintained by `competence-map`. **Load the rows for this chapter's characters before drafting.**

Who knows what, how deep, and — the column that does the work — **where the edge is**. Read as a
grid rather than per profile: the useful facts are distributional. Does anyone here read? Who is
the only one who can fight? What does this cast collectively not know?

> **The rule the file exists for: an unlisted domain is `none`, not "probably fine."** If a scene
> needs a character to know something absent from their row, either they do not know it — which is
> the scene — or they learned it on the page and it gets added here.

Levels: `none` · `passable` · `professional` · `exceptional` · `best alive`

---

## 1. THE GRID

Tier A and tier B. Tier C walk-ons know their job and nothing beside it — that is their third
stroke in `_extras.md`, and it is their whole map.

| character | domain | level | where the edge is |
|---|---|---|---|

**Budget above `professional`** — MC 3 · other tier A 2 · tier B 1 · tier C 1 (their job). Anything
above `professional` is paid for by a scene showing how it was acquired.

**Two incompetences is a floor.** The interesting ones are *adjacent* to the expertise — the
surgeon who cannot set a bone, the general who has never handled money — because those are the ones
a reader assumes the character has.

## 2. REFERRALS — who they ask when it runs out

Delegation reads as competence, not weakness. A character who knows their own boundary and hands
the problem to the right person is more capable than one who improvises a second specialty.

| character | outside their domain, they go to | what asking costs them |
|---|---|---|

The cost is the point: a favour owed, a fee, an admission of ignorance to someone who will remember
it. A free referral is a phone book.

## 3. WHAT NOBODY HERE KNOWS

The cast-wide gaps. This is a plot generator, and a guard against the chapter that quietly invents
a physician because it needs one.

| gap | who in the world does know | what it would cost to reach them |
|---|---|---|

Cross-check against `bible/society.md` (`social-fabric`): who is *allowed* to learn, what costs
money to know, what is guild secret, what is only ever spoken aloud. In a world with 8% literacy,
the number of people who could have read that ledger is a plot constraint.

## 4. BROAD-KNOWLEDGE CHARACTERS

Gods, immortals, cultivators, ASI, bound spirits — minds that genuinely know enormously more than a
person. Empty in most novels; delete the section if this book has none.

| character | kind | what is actually broad | the boundary that still bites | how access is bounded |
|---|---|---|---|---|

`kind`: domain-god · long-lived · artificial · borrowed

**The boundary column is required**, and so is the access column. Bound the *access*, not the
knowledge — the god answers three questions a year, the immortal is asleep or forbidden to
intervene, the ASI speaks through a bad channel, the system charges. An unbounded oracle dissolves
every mystery in the book. Record the same limit in `bible/power-system.md`.

The clause does not leak downward: a world with gods in it is not a world where the farmers know
cosmology, and a sect with an 800-year-old patriarch still has disciples who cannot read.

## 5. BIAS CHECK — run at each arc rollup

Read the `none` and `passable` rows as a block. If the characters who cannot read, cannot fight,
cannot count or cannot cook line up with a gender, a class or a people, the map is encoding a
prejudice (`bias-guard`). The fix is not to make everyone equally able — it is to make sure the
pattern is not the book's argument.
